# Plan

Goal: A shareable, bold-and-playful mortgage payment calculator for a general audience. Users enter home price, down payment, interest rate, loan term, and optional property tax, insurance, and HOA costs, and instantly see their total monthly payment. Below the headline payment, the page shows an amortization breakdown of principal vs. interest over time and a simple year-by-year loan balance summary so users can see how their loan evolves. Results update as inputs change, with clear empty and invalid-input states (e.g., missing or out-of-range numbers) that keep the tool friendly and easy to use.

1. The calculator page shows a form with inputs for home price, down payment, interest rate, and a loan-term select offering 10, 15, 20, and 30 years, plus a down-payment toggle between entering a dollar amount or a percentage, and three optional fields for property tax, insurance, and HOA — all pre-filled with a sensible default scenario (e.g. $400,000 price, $80,000 down, 6.5%, 30-year) so the page shows a real figure on first load, not zeros; all currency amounts are formatted with commas and dollar signs as the user types.
2. Typing in any input updates the headline payment and the breakdown instantly, without pressing a calculate button: with price 400000, down payment 80000, 6.5% over 30 years the headline shows $2,022.62 for principal and interest, and separate small readouts show the monthly tax, insurance, and HOA portions so the total is visibly the sum of its parts; optional fields left blank are treated as $0 rather than blocking the calculation.
3. Invalid or missing inputs keep the tool friendly: a cleared home-price field, a rate outside 0–15%, a negative number in any field, or a down payment greater than the home price each shows an inline message next to the offending field (e.g. 'Enter a home price between $10,000 and $5,000,000'), and the headline area shows a helpful prompt instead of a $0 or NaN figure; fixing the field clears the message immediately.
4. Below the headline, an SVG stacked area chart plots monthly principal paid against monthly interest paid across the whole loan term, with axis labels for years and dollars, a legend distinguishing the two series, and a hover readout showing the exact principal and interest amounts at the month under the pointer.
5. Next to or below the chart, a year-by-year table lists every year of the loan (30 rows for a 30-year term, 15 for 15, 10 for 10) with columns for year, principal paid that year, interest paid that year, and remaining balance, all formatted in dollars, and the table scrolls within a fixed-height container rather than stretching the page.
6. A summary strip states the lifetime totals in plain figures: total interest paid over the loan, total of all payments, and the principal-versus-interest split as a percentage, so a user can see at a glance that a 30-year loan at 6.5% costs roughly $455,000 interest on a $320,000 loan.
7. A 'Save scenario' button (plain, visible, and enabled whenever the inputs are valid) opens a dialog asking for a name, saves the current inputs to the browser database, and the saved scenario appears in a list showing its name, home price, rate, and monthly payment; clicking a saved scenario loads those values back into the form and updates all results, and each saved item has a clearly labelled Delete button to remove it.
8. The app seeds five example scenarios into the database on first load (a 30-year at 6.5%, a 15-year at 5.75%, a low-down-payment first-time-buyer case, a condo with a large HOA fee, and a 10-year aggressive-payoff case), so the saved list is populated and demonstrates the range on a fresh browser.
9. After reloading the page, the inputs last used before the reload are restored and the headline payment, chart, and table show the same figures as before — the working scenario persists in the browser database, not in a URL or a server.
10. A 'Reset' button restores all inputs to the calculator's defaults and clears the headline back to the default-scenario figures, without deleting saved scenarios.
11. Every control works from the keyboard: all inputs are reachable by Tab in a logical order, the loan-term select and down-payment toggle respond to arrow keys, and pressing Enter inside any field keeps focus and updates results; no action requires pointer-only interaction such as hovering.
12. On a phone-sized viewport (375px wide) the form stacks in a single column, the chart scales to the container width without horizontal scrolling, and the year table remains readable with its columns intact.
13. The monthly-payment math handles the boundary cases correctly and visibly: a 0% interest rate produces a straight-line payment of principal divided by months, a down payment equal to the home price shows a $0 loan payment with the chart and table collapsing gracefully rather than dividing by zero, and a rate of exactly 15% (the stated maximum) still renders a valid schedule.
14. The headline payment is the largest element on the page with the secondary readouts, summary strip, and chart clearly subordinate to it; when a saved scenario is loaded, that item is visually highlighted in the saved list until the inputs change, so the user can see which scenario they are looking at.

These are the outcomes this task is judged against.
